After the Achievement School District

After 15 years of state takeovers and charter conversions, Tennessee is shutting down the ASD and launching a new three-tiered intervention system for 2026-27.

The new system needs what the ASD never had: an early warning signal that identifies struggling schools before they fail.

VERA is that signal.

The Lesson

What the ASD Got Wrong

15 Years. Billions Spent. Schools Still Struggling.

The Achievement School District was created to turn around Tennessee's lowest-performing schools through state takeover and charter conversion. It was shut down by overwhelming legislative vote (75-15) in 2025.

The last remaining ASD schools — Hillcrest High and Kirby Middle in Memphis — return to local district control at the start of 2026-27.

The fundamental flaw: The ASD intervened after schools were already in crisis. There was no infrastructure to detect problems before they became failures, and no mechanism to verify whether interventions were actually reaching students.

The New System

Three-Tiered Intervention Model

Tennessee's new accountability framework launches 2026-27. Schools are assigned to tiers based on performance, with annual review to move up or down.

Tier 1: Least Intervention

Schools first identified for intervention start here.

  • District chooses evidence-based intervention strategy
  • OR partners with turnaround expert
  • Annual review determines if school moves to Tier 2

Tier 2: Moderate Intervention

Schools that don't improve at Tier 1 escalate here.

  • Convert to charter school
  • Transfer to higher education institution
  • Replace some/all leadership and staff
  • Intervention committee with turnaround expert

Tier 3: Most Intensive

Last resort for schools that continue to struggle.

  • School closure
  • Complete replacement of leadership and staff
  • State-directed restructuring
